## I. The Roux

In 1755, the British Crown expelled the Acadians from Nova Scotia. They called it *le Grand Dérangement* — the Great Disruption. Families scattered. Homes burned. A people erased from the land they'd worked for generations.

They landed in a swamp nobody wanted. Louisiana. Hot, hostile, forgotten.

And they did what no colonial power expected: they **stayed**. They made crawfish into cuisine. They turned cypress into shelter. They kept speaking French when the Americans told them to stop.

They were *tête dure*. Hardheaded. And the hardheadedness wasn't a flaw. It was the survival mechanism.

> "Mais, it still works, so why you gonna throw it away?"
> — Every Cajun grandmother, ever

Two hundred and seventy years later, that instinct built a blockchain.

## II. The Pot

A Magnalite pot is a cast aluminum vessel they stopped manufacturing decades ago. Thick-walled. Uneven. Dented from years of use. Your grandmother had one. Her mother had one before her.

They conduct heat wrong by every engineering standard. And nothing else on earth cooks like them.

The hot spots aren't defects — they're **features**. The roux darkens differently in different parts of the pot. The gumbo develops complexity that a uniform surface cannot produce. The imperfections are where the flavor lives.

RustChain is a Magnalite pot.

The hardware is old, uneven, dented from decades of use. A Power Mac G4 from 2003. A G5 with 6 gigs of RAM. An IBM POWER8 pulled from a decommissioned datacenter. A Nintendo 64 from 1996 running a neural network that shouldn't fit.

Every machine is a hot spot. Every architecture contributes differently. The imperfections are where the value lives.

## III. The Gumbo

Gumbo doesn't have a recipe. It has a **method**.

You start with the roux — oil and flour, stirred constantly, never walking away, until it reaches the color of a dirty penny. If you stop for ten seconds, it burns and you start over.

Then you add what you have. Andouille if you have it. Chicken if you don't. Shrimp if the boat came in. Okra or filé — *never both*. The constraint isn't a limitation. It's what makes each pot unique.

This is non-bijunctive cooking. You prune what doesn't work. You amplify what does. The result is different every time because the inputs are different every time, and the cook adapts.

A uniform pot makes uniform food. A Magnalite pot makes *gumbo*.

## IV. The Fire

They said AI would flatten everything. Homogenize every voice into corporate slop. They weren't wrong — most of it did. But the answer was never to stop cooking. The answer was to cook *different*.

They said blockchain was dead. Speculation, scams, vaporware. They weren't wrong about most of it either. But somewhere in the noise, a man with a traumatic brain injury sat at a Magnalite pot and stirred. Not for a whitepaper committee. Not for a VC pitch. Because the roux was turning the color of a dirty penny and he wasn't going to walk away.

RustChain doesn't prove mathematical properties. It proves **physical existence**. Every miner attests not with computation but with presence — clock drift from aging oscillators, cache timing from real silicon, thermal curves that no emulator can fake. The machine proves it is real. The machine proves it is old. The machine proves it is *still here*.

**Proof of Antiquity: the machine still works, so why would you throw it away?**

A Nintendo 64 submits attestations to a live blockchain. It runs a neural network on a MIPS processor from 1996. It earns tokens. Not because it's efficient. Because it **exists**. Because someone kept it running. Because survival has value that efficiency metrics cannot capture.

# Appendix: Why Old Computers Outmine New Ones

*The technical case, for the whitepaper-committee types. We love you too.*

## The problem with every other consensus mechanism

**Proof-of-Work** rewards whoever can buy the newest ASICs — hardware depreciates the moment you rack it, so the network is a treadmill of e-waste. **Proof-of-Stake** rewards whoever already has the most tokens — capital, not computation. Both converge on the same failure: the physical machine is disposable.

RustChain inverts the incentive. **Proof of Antiquity** rewards hardware for *surviving*. The multiplier table is the whole thesis in one place:

| Hardware | Base multiplier |
|---|---|
| PowerPC G4 (2003) | 2.5x |
| PowerPC G5 (2005) | 2.0x |
| PowerPC G3 | 1.8x |
| Vintage x86 (486/Pentium/K6) | 1.4x |
| IBM POWER8 | 1.5x |
| Apple Silicon | 1.2x |
| Modern x86_64 | 1.0x |
| Exotic vintage (ARM2, SPARC, MIPS R-series) | up to 4.0x |

A PowerBook G4 from a pawn shop outearns a Threadripper. And because **all hardware becomes old**, every miner's multiplier grows over time. It is the only network where your machine appreciates.

## Why you can't fake old

The obvious attack is emulation: spin up a thousand SheepShaver VMs, claim a thousand G4s. Proof of Antiquity's answer is that age is *physical*, and physics doesn't virtualize. Every attestation must pass hardware fingerprint checks:

1. **Clock-skew and oscillator drift** — quartz oscillators drift as silicon ages; a 20-year-old G4 has a drift signature no fresh CPU or VM reproduces. VMs show hypervisor-flattened timing.
2. **Cache timing tone** — L1/L2/L3 latency sweeps produce a harmonic profile; caches age unevenly and emulators produce impossibly perfect curves.
3. **SIMD unit identity** — AltiVec/SSE/NEON pipelines have per-die timing bias; software emulation flattens it instantly.
4. **Thermal drift entropy** — heat curves through cold boot, saturation, and relaxation are physical and unique; simulators ignore them.
5. **Instruction path jitter** — cycle-level jitter across integer, branch, FPU, and load/store units; no VM replicates real jitter at nanosecond scale.
6. **Anti-emulation behavioral checks** — hypervisor scheduling artifacts, time dilation, uniform thermal response, DMI/SMBIOS traces. VMs still mine, at roughly one-billionth the reward. Honesty is cheaper than fraud.

Emulated vintage machines are additionally caught by **ROM fingerprint clustering**: real hardware has ROM manufacturing variants; emulator farms all boot the same pirated dump. Identical ROM hashes across "different" machines is a confession.

This is verification by physics, not by trust. There is no TEE vendor to compromise, no oracle to bribe. The silicon *is* the credential.

## The environmental argument

Every blockchain claims green credentials. RustChain's version is arithmetic, not offsets: **the greenest computation is the one that already exists.** A G4 kept out of a landfill has its manufacturing carbon already sunk; the marginal cost of its participation is a few watts. Meanwhile the network *pays people to preserve hardware* — 16+ machines and counting rescued from e-waste, tracked publicly at [rustchain.org/preserved.html](https://rustchain.org/preserved.html).

DePIN networks sell coverage, storage, or FLOPS. RustChain sells the one thing the others destroy as a side effect: **continuity of physical computing history.**
